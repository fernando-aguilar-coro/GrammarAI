Hello there! Today, we are going to level up your English by moving beyond simple words like said and told. To sound more professional and precise, we use nuanced reporting verbs like suggest, advise, warn, and deny. Each of these comes with its own special grammatical pattern.

Let’s start with suggest, pronounced suh-JEST. You can use it with an ing-form, like she suggested leaving early, or with a that-clause. A quick tip: in formal English, we often use the base form of the verb in that-clause, such as, he suggested that she be present. Remember, never use an infinitive after suggest. Don't say he suggested to go; instead, say he suggested going.

Next is advise, pronounced ad-VAIZ. This verb always needs an object. You must say, I advised him to wait. If you just say, she advised to take the test, it sounds incomplete to a native speaker. Always add the person you are advising, like, she advised him to take the test.

Then we have warn, pronounced WORN. This is used for danger or prohibitions. You can use it with an object and an infinitive, like, they warned us not to touch it. A common mistake is adding extra prepositions. Keep it simple: warn someone to do something, not warn someone about to do something.

Finally, we have deny, pronounced dih-NAI. This is used when you reject the truth of an accusation. Use it with an ing-form, like, he denied breaking the window. A very common mistake is saying he denied to steal the money. That is incorrect! Always use the gerund form, so say, he denied stealing the money.

To recap, watch your patterns carefully. Suggest takes a gerund or a that-clause. Advise always needs an object before the infinitive. Warn uses an object followed by an infinitive, and deny uses a gerund.

Mastering these patterns will make your English sound much more natural and sophisticated. Keep practicing these structures, and you will notice a real difference in your communication. You are doing great, so keep going!
