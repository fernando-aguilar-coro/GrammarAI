Welcome back to your English lesson! Today, we are completing the second half of the English alphabet, from the letter N all the way to Z, and practicing how to spell everyday words with confidence.

Let us start with our first group of letters.

First is N, as in notebook. Make sure your tongue touches the roof of your mouth for that smooth N sound.

Next is O, a vowel, as in orange.

Then comes P, as in pen. Push a little puff of air out when you say P.

Next is Q, as in question. Remember, in English, the letter Q is almost always followed by the letter U.

After Q, we have R, as in rain, and S, as in sun.

Now, let us move to the second group, starting with T.

T is for table.

Next is U, another vowel, as in umbrella.

Then we have V, as in voice. To pronounce V correctly, rest your top teeth gently on your bottom lip and vibrate your vocal cords. This is different from the letter B, where both lips touch.

Next is W, as in water.

Then comes X, which you hear clearly at the end of words like box.

After X is Y, as in yellow.

And finally, we reach Z, as in zebra. In American English, this letter is called zee, while in British English, it is pronounced zed. Both are completely correct!

Now that you know all twenty-six letters, let us talk about spelling in real-life conversations.

When you do not know how to spell a word, or when someone needs your name, you can ask, How do you spell that? Or, Could you spell your name, please?

When spelling aloud, if a word has two of the same letters together, you can say double. For example, to spell the word coffee, you can say C, O, double F, double E.

Keep practicing by spelling simple objects around your room, like desk, window, or phone. You are making fantastic progress. See you in the next lesson!
