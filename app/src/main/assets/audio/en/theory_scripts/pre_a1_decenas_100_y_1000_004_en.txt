Hello everyone! Welcome back to your English lesson. Today, we are going to master counting larger numbers and talking about prices, focusing on tens, hundreds, and thousands. By the end of this lesson, you will feel confident buying items, discussing costs, and saying big numbers smoothly.

Let us start with the tens: twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, and ninety. Pay special attention to the spelling of forty—it is spelled f-o-r-t-y, with no letter u.

Now, here is a very important pronunciation tip. Learners often confuse numbers like thirteen and thirty, or fourteen and forty. To make your tens sound clear, put the stress on the first syllable. Say THIR-ty, FOR-ty, and FIF-ty. For teen numbers, the stress goes at the end, like thir-TEEN and for-TEEN. Giving that strong accent on the first part makes a huge difference!

When you combine a ten and a single digit, just join them together smoothly, like twenty-three, fifty-six, or ninety-nine.

Next, let us move to a hundred and a thousand.

When you give an exact count, the words hundred and thousand stay singular. So, you say three hundred people, not three hundreds people. The same rule applies to money: say five thousand dollars, never five thousands dollars. We only add an s when speaking generally, such as thousands of people or hundreds of ideas.

In British English, speakers usually add the word and between hundreds and tens. For example, one hundred and fifty. In American English, people often leave out the word and and simply say one hundred fifty. Both are completely correct, so choose whichever feels more natural to you!

Finally, let us look at prices. Understanding prices in English is essential for everyday conversations.

If an item costs forty-five dollars and fifty cents, you can say, forty-five dollars and fifty cents. But in daily speech, native speakers usually shorten this. They simply say, forty-five fifty.

What about larger prices, like one thousand five hundred dollars? You can say, one thousand five hundred dollars, or you might hear people say, fifteen hundred dollars. Both ways are very common.

Let us review a quick practical example. Imagine you are at a store and ask, How much is this coat? The shop assistant might reply, It is one hundred and twenty dollars. Or casually, It is a hundred and twenty.
