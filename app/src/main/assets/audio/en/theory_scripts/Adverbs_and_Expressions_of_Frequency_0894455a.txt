Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to master adverbs and expressions of frequency. These are the handy words and phrases we use to describe how often an action occurs, and they are essential for making your English sound natural and precise.

First, let's look at where these adverbs go in a sentence. Their position depends entirely on the type of verb you are using. 

If you are using the verb to be, such as am, is, are, was, or were, you must place the adverb after the verb. For example, we say, She is always punctual. 

However, if you are using a normal action verb, the adverb goes before the verb. For instance, They usually arrive early. 

Finally, if you have an auxiliary or helping verb, like have, has, or will, place the adverb right in the middle, between the auxiliary and the main verb. For example, We have rarely seen such beauty.

Now, let's look at the frequency scale, moving from one hundred percent down to zero percent, along with some helpful pronunciation tips. 

At the very top, at one hundred percent, we have always. 

At eighty percent, we use usually. Listen closely to the pronunciation here: it is yoo-zhoo-uh-lee. 

At sixty percent, we have often. A great tip for this word is that the letter t is silent, so we pronounce it off-en. 

At forty percent, we use sometimes. 

At twenty percent, we have rarely, pronounced rair-lee. 

And at zero percent, we use never.

Sometimes, instead of a single word, we use longer expressions of frequency, such as every day, once a week, or twice a month. Unlike single adverbs, these longer expressions typically go at the very end of the sentence to keep a nice, natural rhythm. For example, you would say, I practice my English every day.

To make sure your English is flawless, let's cover a few common pitfalls to avoid. 

First, remember the rule for the verb to be. Do not say, She always is happy. Instead, say, She is always happy. 

Second, avoid double negatives. Since the word never is already negative, you do not need another negative word in the sentence. Instead of saying, I don't never go there, you should say, I never go there. 

Third, do not confuse frequency with duration. Use always to describe how often you do something, but use all day to describe how long you do it. 

And lastly, keep those longer expressions at the end of your sentence. Avoid putting phrases like every day in the middle of your sentence. 

Keep these simple rules in mind, and you will be expressing yourself with confidence and clarity. Keep practicing, and I will see you in our next lesson!
