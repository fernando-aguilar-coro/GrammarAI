Hello! Today, we are going to master the modal verbs can and can't. These are essential tools for talking about what you are able to do and what is permitted.

First, let's look at the structure. One of the best things about modal verbs is that they never change form. Whether you are talking about I, you, we, they, or he, she, and it, the word can stays exactly the same. You never add an s, even for the third person. Also, always follow can with the bare infinitive, which is just the verb without the word to. For example, you say I can swim, not I can to swim.

When we talk about physical ability, we use can for things we are able to do, like she can play the piano, or birds can fly. If you lack that ability, use can't, which is pronounced with a short a sound, like in cat. For instance, I can't lift this heavy box.

We also use can for permission in informal situations. If you want to ask for something, say, Can I leave the room early? To grant permission, you might say, Yes, you can use my laptop. To deny it, simply say, No, you can't park here.

Now, let’s avoid some common mistakes. Remember, never add an s to can. It is always he can play, not he cans play. Also, never use the word to after can. Just go straight to the verb. When you want to ask a question, don't use the auxiliary verb do. Instead, just move can to the front of the sentence. So, instead of saying does you can help me, simply ask, Can you help me? Finally, always follow can't with the base form of the verb, not an ing form. It is she can't play, not she can't playing.

Practice these rules, and you will sound much more natural in no time. Keep up the great work, and I will see you in the next lesson!
