Hello there! Today, we are going to master the art of giving advice in English using modal verbs. These are special words that help us express our attitude toward an action, whether it is a gentle suggestion or a firm warning.

Let’s start with the most common one: should. We pronounce it with a soft sh sound, like in shoe, followed by a short u. We use should for general advice or opinions. The structure is simple: just use the subject, then should, and then the base form of the verb. For example, you should study harder. Remember, never add the word to after should. It is always you should go, never you should to go.

Next, we have ought to. This one sounds a bit more formal, like in the word caught. We use ought to when we are talking about moral obligations or societal expectations. For instance, citizens ought to respect the law. While it is similar to should, it is less common in questions. If you are asking for advice, it is much more natural to use should instead of the slightly old-fashioned ought I to.

Finally, we have had better. This is for urgent advice or strong warnings where there might be negative consequences if you don't follow the suggestion. Even though it uses the word had, it is strictly for the present or the immediate future. In casual conversation, we almost always use a contraction, saying I’d better or you’d better. For example, you’d better leave now, or you’ll be late. 

A very common mistake here is the negative form. Remember, we place the word not after better, not after had. So, say you had better not go, rather than you hadn't better go. Also, avoid saying have better or should better; always stick to the fixed phrase had better.

To recap: use should for general suggestions, ought to for moral duties, and had better when you really need to emphasize that someone should act quickly to avoid trouble. Practice these in your daily conversations, and you will sound much more natural and precise. Keep up the great work, and I will see you in the next lesson!
