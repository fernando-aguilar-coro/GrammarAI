Hello there! Today, we are going to master the art of reported speech, which is how we tell someone what another person said without using their exact words. This is often called indirect speech.

When we report something in the past, we use a process called backshifting. This means we move the verb tense one step back into the past. For example, if someone says I work hard in the present simple, we report it as he said that he worked hard in the past simple. If they say I am reading, we report that he was reading. If they say I bought a car, we shift that past simple into the past perfect, saying he had bought a car. The same applies to the present perfect, so I have finished becomes he had finished. For modals, will changes to would, and can changes to could.

When reporting statements, you can use the verbs said or told. Remember that told always needs an object, like told me or told them. You can use the word that to connect your sentences, but it is optional.

For questions, the structure changes. We stop using the question word order and switch to a standard affirmative sentence. For yes or no questions, use if or whether. For example, are you coming becomes he asked if I was coming. For questions starting with who, what, or where, keep that word but change the order. Instead of asking where do you live, you would say she asked me where I lived.

When reporting commands or requests, use an infinitive phrase. For positive commands, use to plus the verb, like the teacher told us to sit down. For negative commands, use not to, such as he warned me not to touch that button.

Finally, remember to adjust your time and place words. Now becomes then, today becomes that day, and yesterday becomes the day before. Here changes to there, and this becomes that.

Watch out for these common mistakes: don't forget to backshift your verbs, and remember that in reported questions, the subject comes before the verb. Also, remember that we say he told me, not he said me. Lastly, for commands, always use the infinitive to, not an ing form.

Keep practicing these shifts, and you will sound much more natural when sharing stories or relaying information. You are doing great!
