Hello and welcome back, English learners! Today, we are mastering two powerful tenses for talking about the future: the Future Continuous and the Future Perfect. Both describe future events, but they focus on completely different aspects of time.

Let's start with the Future Continuous. We form this tense using will be, plus the main verb ending in i-n-g, pronounced smoothly as will be doing. 

We use the Future Continuous for an action that will be in progress, or actively happening, at a specific moment in the future. The focus here is on duration and continuation.

In positive sentences, you can say, "I will be working," or "She will be traveling." 
In negative sentences, change will to won't: "I won't be working," or "She won't be traveling." 
To ask a question, flip the word order: "Will you be working?" or "Will she be traveling?"

This tense is ideal for interrupted future events, such as: "I will be waiting for you when your train arrives." It is also great for parallel future actions: "Tonight, I will be studying while he will be cooking."

Now, let's look at the Future Perfect. We form this using will have, plus the past participle of the main verb, pronounced as will have done.

Unlike the continuous tense, the Future Perfect focuses on completion and final results. It tells us that an action will be finished before a specific deadline or time in the future.

For positive statements, you can say, "I will have finished," or "They will have left." 
For negative statements, say, "I won't have finished," or "They won't have left." 
And for questions, ask, "Will you have finished?" or "Will they have left?"

Notice how we often pair this tense with time markers using the word by. For example: "By five p.m., I will have completed the report." We can also use it to measure duration up to a future point: "Next year, we will have lived here for ten years."

To make sure you master these tenses, keep four common mistakes in mind.

First, do not use the Future Continuous for permanent future states. Use the simple future instead. Say, "Next year I will live in Paris permanently," rather than "will be living."

Second, do not confuse ongoing events with deadlines. If you are talking about a completion date, use the Future Perfect. Say, "By tomorrow, I will have finished the project," not "I will be finishing."

Third, never leave out the auxiliary verb have. Always say, "By next week, she will have completed the course," not "she will completed."

And finally, watch your time clauses starting with words like when, if, or by the time. Use the present tense inside the time clause itself. Say, "By the time you arrive, I will have cooked," not "By the time you will arrive."

Keep practicing these distinctions, and you will sound far more precise and fluent in your advanced spoken English. Excellent job today, and I will see you in our next lesson!
