Hello there! Today, we are going to master the difference between two very important words: for and since. These are essential when you want to talk about how long something has been happening.

Let’s start with the word for, which is pronounced like f-or. We use for to describe the duration of an action, or the total amount of time that has passed. Think of it as answering the question, How long?

When you are talking about seconds or minutes, you might say, I have been waiting for ten minutes. If you are talking about hours or days, you could say, She has worked here for five years. And for longer periods like months or years, you might say, They have lived abroad for a long time. The formula here is simple: use your subject, the auxiliary verb have or has, the past participle, and then add for followed by the duration.

Next, let’s look at since, pronounced like s-ins. While for measures the total time, since tells us the exact starting point. It answers the question, Since when?

For example, if you are talking about a specific clock time, you would say, I have been studying since 8:00 AM. If you are referring to days, dates, or years, you could say, He has known her since Monday, or he has known her since 2015. You can even use it with life events, like saying, We have been friends since we were children. The formula is the same as before, but you replace the duration with that specific starting point.

Now, let’s avoid a few common mistakes. First, remember not to mix them up. Never say, I have studied since three hours. Because three hours is a duration, you must use for. So, say, I have studied for three hours.

Second, be careful with your verb tenses. Since works best with the present perfect tense, which connects the past to the present. Avoid using it with the simple past for actions that are already finished.

Finally, don't confuse for with the word during. Use for when you are measuring a specific amount of time, like for two weeks, rather than just describing an event that happened within a timeframe.

Keep practicing these, and you will find that talking about time becomes second nature. You are doing a great job, so keep it up!
