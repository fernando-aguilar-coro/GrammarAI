Hello there! Today we are going to look at a fascinating part of English grammar: verbs that change their meaning depending on whether they are followed by a gerund, which is the verb-ing form, or an infinitive, which is to plus the verb.

Let us start with remember and forget. When you use a gerund, you are looking back at a past memory. For example, I remember locking the door means the locking happened first, and now I am recalling it. But if you use an infinitive, you are looking forward to a duty. Remember to lock the door means you have an obligation to do it in the future.

Next, let us talk about the verb stop. This one is very important. If you say he stopped smoking, it means he quit the habit entirely. But if you say he stopped to smoke, it means he paused what he was doing, perhaps walking or driving, specifically so he could have a cigarette. The infinitive here shows the purpose of the pause.

Then we have regret. Using a gerund expresses remorse for something you already did, like I regret telling him the truth. However, we use the infinitive with formal announcements, such as we regret to inform you that your application was denied. It sounds much more professional.

Finally, consider the verb try. If you try adding more salt to your soup, you are experimenting to see how it tastes. But if you try to finish your report by five o'clock, you are making a real, earnest effort to accomplish a difficult task.

A common mistake is thinking these forms are interchangeable, but they really are not. Remember, the gerund usually points to the past or an ongoing action, while the infinitive points to the future or a specific purpose.

To get these right, pay attention to the timing of the action. Are you recalling a memory, or are you planning a task? Are you ending a habit, or are you taking a break to do something else? Keep practicing these distinctions, and you will sound much more natural and precise in your English. You are doing a great job, so keep at it!
