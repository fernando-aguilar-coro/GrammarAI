Hello there! Today, we are going to master a very important part of English grammar: verbs followed by the to-infinitive.

In English, certain verbs act as a bridge, requiring the next action to be in the to-infinitive form. This means you use the word to followed by the base form of the verb. We use this pattern when we talk about intentions, future decisions, or our ability to do something.

Think of the structure like this: you start with your subject, add your main verb, and then follow it immediately with to plus the base verb. For example, you would say, She decided to leave.

Let’s look at three main categories where this happens. First, we have verbs of decision and intention, like decide, plan, hope, and intend. Because these actions are looking toward the future, we use the to-infinitive. For instance, They decided to postpone the meeting.

Second, we have verbs of capacity and limitation, such as afford, manage, and fail. These describe what we are or are not able to do. A common example is, We cannot afford to buy a new car.

Third, we have verbs of attitude and response, like refuse, agree, and promise. These show your commitment to an action. You might say, He refused to sign the contract.

Now, let’s watch out for a few common mistakes. First, remember not to use the ing form after these verbs. It is decided to leave, not decided leaving. Second, don’t forget the word to! It is very common to accidentally say I want go, but the correct way is I want to go.

Also, be careful with object placement. You cannot say she refused me to help. Instead, simply say she refused to help. Finally, remember that the to in an infinitive is just a marker, not a preposition. It must be followed by a base verb, never an ing form. So, it is to go, never to going.

Keep practicing these patterns, and you will sound more natural and confident in no time. Great work today!
