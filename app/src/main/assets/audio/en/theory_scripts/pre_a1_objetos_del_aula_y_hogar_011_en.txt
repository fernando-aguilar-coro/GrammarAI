Hello everyone, and welcome to today's English lesson! I am so excited to help you build your vocabulary. Today, we are going to learn five essential everyday objects that you will find both in the classroom and at home.

Let's start with our very first word: Book. Notice the short double O sound in book, pronounced just like the word good. B-O-O-K. In a sentence, you can say, I am reading an interesting book, or in the plural form, There are many books on the shelf.

Next up, we have Pen. Pay close attention to the vowel sound here. It is a short E sound: Pen. Try not to confuse it with the word pin, which has an I sound. A pen uses ink to write. For example, you might ask a friend, Can I borrow your pen, please?

Our third word is Table. Notice how the first syllable has a long A sound, like the letter A itself: TAY-bul. A table is a flat surface with legs where we put things or eat meals. For example, The laptop is on the table. When talking about more than one, simply add an S to make tables.

Moving on to our fourth word: Chair. This word starts with the CH sound, which sounds like the beginning of the word chocolate. CH-air. A chair is what you sit on. In a full sentence, you could say, Please take a seat on this comfortable chair.

Finally, our fifth word is Door. The double O in door creates a long OR sound, pronounced like floor or more. DOOR. We use doors to enter or leave a room. For instance, you can say, Please close the door behind you.

Notice how we use the word "a" before all of these singular objects because they all start with consonant sounds: a book, a pen, a table, a chair, and a door.

Try looking around your room right now. Can you point to a chair or a table and name it aloud in English? Practice saying these five words today, and try making your own simple sentences with them. Keep up the fantastic work, and I will see you in our next lesson!
