Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to explore two very important types of pronouns: reflexive pronouns and reciprocal pronouns. Understanding the difference between them will instantly make your English sound more precise and natural.

Let us start with reflexive pronouns, pronounced rih-FLEK-siv pronouns. Think of a mirror. When you look into a mirror, your reflection bounces right back to you. In the same way, a reflexive pronoun shows that the action of the verb reflects back onto the person performing it. 

Here are the forms you need to know. 
For the first person, we use myself for one person, and ourselves for more than one. 
For the second person, we say yourself when speaking to one person, but yourselves when addressing a group. Notice how that ending changes from dash-self to dash-selves in the plural!
For the third person singular, we have himself for males, herself for females, and itself for objects or animals. When we refer to a group, we use themselves.

You can use these in a few key ways. First, as a direct object: She cut herself while cooking. Second, after a preposition: He bought a gift for himself. And third, for emphasis: The manager himself signed the contract.

Now, let us move to reciprocal pronouns, pronounced rih-SIP-ruh-kul pronouns. These are used when two or more people perform the exact same action toward one another. The relationship is mutual, going back and forth like a tennis ball. 

There are two main forms: each other, and one another. Traditionally, we use each other for two parties, and one another for three or more. For example: John and Mary looked at each other. That means John looked at Mary, and Mary looked at John. Or, for a larger group: The team members supported one another throughout the tournament. 

Now, let us look at some common pitfalls so you can avoid them easily.

First, notice the huge difference in meaning between reflexive and reciprocal pronouns. If you say, They looked at themselves, it means each person looked in a mirror at their own reflection. But if you say, They looked at each other, it means they made eye contact.

Second, remember that reciprocal pronouns always require a plural subject. You cannot say, He talked to each other. You need two or more people interacting.

Third, never use a reflexive pronoun as the subject of a sentence. Avoid saying, Myself and John went to the store. Instead, say, John and I went to the store. 

Finally, always match the gender and number of the subject. If the subject is singular and female, she hurt herself, never she hurt themselves.

Keep these simple distinctions in mind, practice them aloud, and you will master these pronouns in no time. You are doing fantastic work, so keep it up!
