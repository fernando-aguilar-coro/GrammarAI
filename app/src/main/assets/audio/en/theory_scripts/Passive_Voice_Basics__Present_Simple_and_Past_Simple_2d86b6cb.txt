Hello there! Today, we are diving into the passive voice. We use the passive voice when we want to focus on the action itself or the person or thing receiving that action, rather than the person doing it. It is a fantastic tool to use when the performer of the action is unknown, irrelevant, or already understood.

To build a passive sentence, we follow a simple rule: we use the verb to be, conjugated in the correct tense, followed by the past participle of the main verb.

Let’s look at how this works in the present and past simple. 

In the present simple, we use am, is, or are, plus the past participle. For example, you might say, The car is washed every week. Notice how we use is because the car is singular.

In the past simple, we switch to was or were, plus the past participle. For instance, The car was washed yesterday. 

When you are converting a sentence from active to passive, remember these three steps. First, identify the direct object of the active sentence; this becomes the new subject of your passive sentence. Second, choose your auxiliary verb carefully—use is or are for the present, and was or were for the past, making sure it matches your new subject. Third, you can choose to leave out the original performer entirely, or add them at the end using the word by.

Now, let’s watch out for a few common pitfalls. 

First, be careful with your auxiliary verbs. A very common mistake is using was with a plural subject. Remember, we say the books were printed, not the books was printed. 

Second, never forget to use the past participle. It is easy to accidentally use the base form, but remember that we say English is spoken here, not English is speak here. 

Third, keep in mind that you can only make passive sentences with verbs that take an object. You cannot make a passive sentence with verbs like happen, arrive, or sleep, because these verbs do not have a direct object to move to the front of the sentence.

Practice these structures, and you will find your English sounding much more professional and precise. Keep up the great work!
