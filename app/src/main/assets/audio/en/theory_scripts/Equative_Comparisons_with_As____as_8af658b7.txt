Hello there! Today, we are going to master a very useful way to compare things in English: the equative comparison. We use this structure when we want to say that two people, objects, or ideas share the exact same quality.

The secret is simple: just use the word as, followed by an adjective or adverb, and then another as. Think of it like a sandwich, with the adjective or adverb tucked right in the middle.

Let’s start with adjectives. If you want to compare two things, you place the base form of the adjective between the two as words. For example, you could say, The red car is as fast as the blue car, or, She is as tall as her brother. Notice how we keep the adjective in its simple, base form.

We can do the exact same thing with adverbs to describe how an action is performed. For instance, you might say, He speaks English as fluently as a native speaker, or, Mark runs as quickly as David.

Now, what if things are not equal? To show a difference, just add the word not before the first as. You can say, Chemistry is not as difficult as physics, or, She does not work as efficiently as her manager. You might occasionally hear people use so instead of the first as, like not so difficult as, but using as is much more common and natural.

You can also use this structure to show exact multiples. If you want to say something is double the size, you can say, This house is twice as large as our old apartment.

Before we wrap up, let’s look at a few common mistakes to avoid. First, never use comparative or superlative forms. Never say as taller as or as tallest as; always stick to the base form, like as tall as. Second, don't forget the second as! It is a pair, so you must include both. Finally, remember that we use as with adjectives and adverbs, not just nouns. If you want to compare quantities, you would use as much or as many, but that is a lesson for another day.
