Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to master two of the most fundamental building blocks of the English language: subject pronouns and the verb to be. 

First, let's look at subject pronouns. These are the words we use to replace nouns when they are the subject of a sentence. In the singular, we have: I, for yourself; You, for the person you are speaking to; and He, She, or It, for a third person or object. In the plural, we use We, for a group that includes yourself; You, when speaking to more than one person; and They, for other people or things.

Now, let's pair these pronouns with the most essential, irregular verb in English: the verb to be, which is pronounced bee. In the simple present tense, this verb changes form depending on the subject. 

Let's start with the affirmative, or positive, forms. For the first person singular, we say: I am. For the second person, we say: You are. For the third person singular, we say: He is, She is, or It is. For the plural forms, we say: We are, and They are.

To make these sentences negative, we simply add the word not after the verb. So, we say: I am not, You are not, He, She, or It is not, We are not, and They are not.

When we want to ask a direct question, we swap the order of the pronoun and the verb. This is called inversion. Instead of saying the pronoun first, we start with the verb. For example, we ask: Am I? Are you? Is he, she, or it? Are we? and Are they?

In everyday spoken English, we usually blend these words together to speak more quickly and naturally. These are called contractions. For example, I am becomes I'm. You are becomes you're. He is, she is, and it is become he's, she's, and it's. We are becomes we're, and they are becomes they're. For negative contractions, we often say isn't or aren't, such as he isn't or they aren't.

To wrap up our lesson, let's look at a few common mistakes to avoid. 

First, always match the correct verb form with the pronoun. Never say "He are." The correct form is always "He is."

Second, remember that English sentences almost always require a subject pronoun. You cannot omit it. For example, instead of saying "Is beautiful today," you must say "It is beautiful today."

Third, be careful with spelling. Do not confuse the contraction "it's", spelled with an apostrophe, which means "it is", with the possessive word "its", spelled without an apostrophe, which shows ownership.

Finally, remember to invert the subject and verb for questions. Instead of saying "You are ready?" with a rising tone, the grammatically correct question is "Are you ready?"
