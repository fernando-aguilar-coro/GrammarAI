Hello and welcome! Today, we are mastering one of the most useful tools in English for talking about the future: the modal auxiliary verb will. 

The great thing about will is that it never changes its form, no matter who is doing the action. Whether you are talking about I, you, he, she, or they, the modal stays exactly the same.

Let's look at how we build sentences with will. 

For positive statements, you take your subject, add will, and follow it with the base form of the verb. For example: I will help you with the bags. In everyday conversation, we often contract this to: I'll help you.

For negative statements, you add not after will. While you can say will not, native speakers almost always use the contraction won't. So you get: She won't accept the offer. Pay special attention to the pronunciation of won't. It has a round o sound, like the word home, which helps you avoid confusing it with the word want.

To ask a question, simply switch the order. Put will at the beginning, followed by the subject and the base verb. For instance: Will you attend the meeting?

Now, when exactly should you use will? There are three main situations.

First, use will for spontaneous decisions. These are choices you make right at the moment of speaking without planning ahead. Imagine your phone rings. You do not have a prior plan, so you immediately say: The phone is ringing. I will answer it.

Second, use will to make promises and commitments. If a friend tells you something private, you can reassure them by saying: I will keep your secret safe.

Third, use will for predictions based on your personal opinions, beliefs, or guesses. You will often hear will paired with verbs like think, believe, or hope. For example: I think it will rain tomorrow.

Before we finish, let us review a few common pitfalls to make sure your English stays polished and natural.

First, never put the word to after will. We say I will go, never I will to go.

Second, never add an s to will for third-person subjects. He wills come is incorrect; always say He will come.

Finally, remember the difference between will and going to. If you already planned an event before speaking, do not use will. Likewise, if you see immediate physical evidence right in front of your eyes, choose going to instead. If the sky is filled with dark, heavy storm clouds, say: Look at those clouds, it is going to rain, rather than it will rain.

Keep practicing these patterns, and you will speak about the future with absolute confidence. Keep up the fantastic work!
