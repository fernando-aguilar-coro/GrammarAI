Hello there! Today, we are going to master the Present Perfect Continuous tense. You might also hear this called the Present Perfect Progressive. It is a fantastic tool for connecting the past to the present, especially when you want to emphasize how long something has been happening.

To build this tense, you need three parts: the auxiliary verb have or has, the word been, and your main verb ending in -ing. 

In an affirmative sentence, you would say, for example, I have been working. If you want to make it negative, just add not, like, she has not been sleeping. And for questions, move the auxiliary to the front: Have you been waiting long?

We use this tense for two main reasons. First, for unfinished actions that started in the past and are still happening now. For example, we have been living in this city for five years. Second, for recently finished actions where you can still see the result. If you see someone out of breath, you might ask, have you been running?

To help you sound more natural, keep an eye on your time markers. Use for when you are talking about a duration, like for two hours. Use since when you are pointing to a specific starting point, like since nine o'clock. You can also use lately or recently to describe ongoing habits.

Now, let’s avoid a few common pitfalls. First, remember that we never use stative verbs like know, want, or belong in the continuous form. If you want to say you have known someone for years, just use the standard Present Perfect: I have known him for years. 

Second, don't forget the word been! It is the bridge between your auxiliary and your main verb. Finally, try not to confuse for and since. Just remember: for is for the length of time, and since is for the starting point on the calendar or clock.

Practice saying these out loud, focusing on the rhythm of the -ing ending. You are doing a great job, and with a little bit of practice, using the Present Perfect Continuous will become second nature to you. Keep going!
