Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to explore a fantastic tense that helps you talk about all the adventures, milestones, and memories in your life: the Present Perfect Simple.

When we talk about life experiences, we use the present perfect because the exact time something happened doesn't matter. What matters is whether you have done it at any point in your life, right up to this very moment. 

To build these sentences, you only need two key ingredients after your subject: the auxiliary verb have or has, followed by the past participle of the main verb, which is often called verb three. 

Let us look at how this works in practice. For the subjects I, you, we, and they, we use have. For example, you can say, I have visited Japan, or they have visited Japan. For third-person singular subjects like he, she, or it, we switch to has. For example, she has flown in a helicopter. Notice how we use the past participle visited for regular verbs, and flown for the irregular verb fly.

Now, to make your conversations sound even more natural, we often use two special words: ever and never. 

We use ever, pronounced eh-ver, mostly in questions to ask if someone has done something at any time in their life. You might ask a friend, Have you ever eaten sushi? 

On the other hand, we use never, pronounced neh-ver, to say that an experience has not happened up to now. For instance, I have never seen the Northern Lights. 

Where do these words go? Always place ever and never directly between your auxiliary verb and the past participle. Think of them as sitting comfortably in the middle, as in: He has never tried scuba diving.

Before we finish, let us review a few common pitfalls so you can avoid them with confidence. 

First, never use specific time markers like yesterday, last year, or in 2010 with this tense. If you want to specify when an event happened, use the simple past instead, like: I visited Paris in 2018. But for an open life experience, simply say: I have visited Paris.

Second, be careful with double negatives. Because the word never is already negative, your auxiliary verb must stay affirmative. Say, I have never been to France, never say, I haven't never been to France.

Finally, make sure you use the past participle, not the simple past. It is always, I have gone, never, I have went.

Practice sharing one amazing thing you have done, and one thing you have never done. You are making great progress, keep practicing, and see you next time!
