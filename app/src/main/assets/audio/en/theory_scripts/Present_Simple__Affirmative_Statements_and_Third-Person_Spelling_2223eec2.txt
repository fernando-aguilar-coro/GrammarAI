Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to master a fundamental part of English grammar: the Present Simple tense in affirmative statements. We use this tense all the time to talk about our daily habits, routines, general truths, and permanent situations. 

Let's start with how we form these sentences. It is actually very simple, but there is one key rule you must remember. 

For the subjects I, you, we, and they, we use the base form of the verb. For example, you would say, "I work in an office." 

However, when the subject is the third-person singular—meaning he, she, or it—we must add an "s" or "es" to the end of the verb. So, instead of "work", we say "works". For example, "She works in an office." Listen closely to that ending sound: works. 

Now, let's look at the spelling rules for the third-person singular, as they can be a bit tricky. 

First, the general rule. For most verbs, you simply add an "s" to the end. For example, "read" becomes "reads", and "play" becomes "plays". 

Second, if a verb ends in a hissing or buzzing sound—like double s, s-h, c-h, x, or z—or if it ends in the letter "o", we add "e-s". This actually adds an extra syllable to the pronunciation of most of these words. For instance, "pass" becomes "passes", "wash" becomes "washes", "watch" becomes "watches", "fix" becomes "fixes", and "go" becomes "goes". 

Third, let's look at verbs that end in the letter "y". If the "y" comes after a consonant, we drop the "y" and add "i-e-s". So, "study" becomes "studies", and "fly" becomes "flies". But be careful! If there is a vowel before the "y", like in the verb "play", we do not change the "y". We simply add an "s" to make "plays". 

To help you speak like a pro, let's look at a few common mistakes to avoid. 

A very common slip-up is forgetting the "s" altogether. You might hear someone say, "He like coffee." Remember to always add that "s" and say, "He likes coffee." 

Another mistake is adding an "s" when the subject is plural. We only add the "s" to the verb for singular subjects. So, we say, "The dogs bark," not "The dogs barks." 

Finally, keep an eye on your spelling. Don't confuse the "y" rules. Remember that "plays" is spelled with a "y-s", not "i-e-s", because of the vowel "a" right before the "y".
