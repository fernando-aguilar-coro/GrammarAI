Hello there! Today, we are going to master how to talk about things we did in the past. We have three main ways to do this: used to, would, and the simple past. Choosing the right one depends on whether you are talking about a habit or a state that has changed.

First, let’s look at used to, which is pronounced like you-st-tuh. We use this for habits or states that have completely stopped. For an affirmative sentence, we say I used to live in London. In the negative, we say she didn't use to like coffee. And for a question, we ask, did you use to play the piano? Notice that in the negative and the question, we drop the d from used and just say use to.

Next, we have would. We use this only for repeated actions or routines, never for states. For example, you can say, every summer, we would go to the beach. But you cannot say I would live in London, because living somewhere is a state, not a repeated action. Use would only when you are reminiscing about past behaviors.

Then, there is the simple past. This is for finished actions at a specific time. Unlike used to or would, it does not focus on repetition. You simply state that something happened, like I went to Paris last year.

Now, let’s avoid some common mistakes. First, remember that would is for actions, not states. If you want to talk about a state, like owning a dog, always use used to. So, say I used to have a dog, not I would have a dog.

Second, watch your spelling. When you use did or didn't, always go back to use to without the d. It is a very common slip-up!

Third, do not use used to for a single, one-time event. If you went to Paris once, just use the simple past. Used to is strictly for things that happened over and over again or for long-term situations that are no longer true today.

Keep practicing these, and you will sound much more natural when telling stories about your past. You are doing great, so keep going!
