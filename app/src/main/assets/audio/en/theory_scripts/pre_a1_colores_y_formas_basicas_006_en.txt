Hello everyone, and welcome to today's English lesson! Today, we are going to talk about something you see all around you every single day: colors and basic shapes. We are also going to learn a fundamental rule in English grammar: how to place adjectives when describing objects.

In English, when we want to describe a person, place, or thing, the descriptive word—known as an adjective—always comes right before the noun. For example, if you want to describe an apple using its color, you say: a red apple. Notice how the word red comes first, followed by the noun, apple. In English, we never put the color after the noun, so you would not say "an apple red."

Let us practice with a few more colorful examples. Think about the sky on a clear day. We say: a blue sky. Think about a fresh leaf on a tree. We say: a green leaf. And for a piece of fruit, we say: a yellow banana. Pay close attention to your pronunciation here. Make sure to hold the smooth, long "oo" sound in blue, and keep the "e" sound short and crisp when you say red.

Now, let us bring basic shapes into the picture! Some of the most common shapes are circles, squares, and triangles. Just like colors, shape words often act as adjectives when they describe an object, which means they also go before the noun. For instance, you can say: a round table, a square box, or a triangular sign.

What happens when you want to use both a color and a shape to describe something? You simply group them together before the noun! For example, you can say: a red circle, or a blue square. If you are describing an everyday object, you might say: I see a small blue square button.

Here is a common mistake to watch out for: remember that in English, adjectives never change to become plural. Even if you have ten apples, the color word stays exactly the same. You say: red apples, never "reds apples." The letter S only goes at the end of the noun, apples, and never on the descriptive word, red.

To practice today, look around the room you are in right now. Pick three objects, identify their color or shape, and say them out loud using this simple rule. For example: a white clock, a square desk, or a green plant.
