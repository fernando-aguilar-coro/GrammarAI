Hello there! Today, we are diving into the Past Continuous tense. This is a fantastic tool for describing actions that were ongoing at a specific moment in the past. To build this tense, we use the past forms of the verb to be—that is, was or were—followed by the main verb with an ing ending.

Let’s look at how we build our sentences. For an affirmative statement, you combine the subject, the auxiliary verb, and the verb-ing. For example, you would say, I was working. When making it negative, just add not, or use the contraction. For instance, he wasn't sleeping. And for questions, simply move the auxiliary to the front, like in the question, Were you listening?

Now, we use the Past Continuous in two main ways. First, for interrupted actions. Think of this as a long, ongoing background action being cut short by a sudden event. We use the Past Continuous for the long action and the Simple Past for the interruption. You can connect these using when or while. For example, I was reading a book when the phone rang. Notice how reading is the long action, and the phone ringing is the sudden interruption.

Second, we use it for simultaneous actions. This happens when two things are happening at the exact same time. In this case, we use the Past Continuous for both parts of the sentence, usually linked by while or as. For example, She was cooking dinner while he was cleaning the garage.

Before you go, here are a few common pitfalls to avoid. First, remember not to use the Past Continuous for single, finished events; stick to the Simple Past for those. Second, watch your subject-verb agreement. Always say they were walking, never they was walking. Third, remember that while is usually followed by the Past Continuous, while when is followed by the Simple Past. Finally, avoid using stative verbs like know, want, or belong in the continuous form. Even if you are talking about a background state, these verbs should stay in their simple form.

Keep practicing these structures, and you will be sounding like a native speaker in no time. You are doing great!
