Hello! Today, we are going to master the art of being polite in English using modal verbs. Choosing the right word can completely change how your request is received, so let’s break down the most important ones.

First, we have Can. It is pronounced /kæn/. This is informal and direct, perfect for friends or peers. For example, you might say, Can you pass the document?

Next is Could, pronounced /kʊd/. This is your go-to for standard, polite requests. It sounds much softer than can. Try saying, Could you review this report by tomorrow?

Then we have May, pronounced /meɪ/. This is very formal and official. Remember, use May only when you are asking for permission for yourself, like, May I be excused from the meeting?

Finally, we have Would, pronounced /wʊd/. This is perfect for invitations or asking for someone's cooperation. A great phrase to practice is, Would you mind sending me the update?

To build these sentences, just follow this simple order: start with your modal verb, add the subject, then the base verb, and finally your complement.

Now, let’s look at a few common traps to avoid. 

First, remember that May is for self-permission. Never say May you open the window, as that sounds unnatural. Use Could or Would instead.

Second, be careful in professional settings. While Can is fine with friends, always use Could or Would in the office to sound more respectful.

Third, watch out for double modals. Never combine them. You should never say Could you might help me. Just pick one modal and stick with it!

Finally, pay attention to the phrase Would you mind. This is a common stumbling block. Always follow it with an ing verb, or gerund. So, say Would you mind closing the door, rather than Would you mind to close the door.

Practice these today, and you will sound much more natural and polite in your English conversations. You are doing great, so keep practicing!
