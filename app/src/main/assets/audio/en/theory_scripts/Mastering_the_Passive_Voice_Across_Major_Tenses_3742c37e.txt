Hello! Today we are going to master the passive voice. We use the passive voice when we want to focus on the action itself, or the person or thing receiving the action, rather than who is actually doing it.

To form the passive voice, remember this simple formula: start with the subject, add the correct form of the verb to be, follow it with the past participle, or V3 form of the verb, and optionally add by followed by the agent.

Let’s look at how this changes across different tenses. In the present simple, we change they write books to books are written by them. If it is happening right now, in the present continuous, we say books are being written by them. Notice how we add the word being here.

Moving to the past, for the past simple, we say books were written by them. For the past continuous, we use books were being written by them. 

When we talk about completed actions, we use the perfect tenses. For the present perfect, it becomes books have been written by them, and for the past perfect, it is books had been written by them. 

Finally, for the future simple, we say books will be written by them, and if we use a modal verb like must, it becomes books must be written by them.

Now, let’s watch out for a few common mistakes. First, don't forget the auxiliary verb. In continuous tenses, you must include the word being. For example, say the car is being repaired, not just the car is repaired. 

Second, remember that only verbs that take an object can be passive. You cannot make intransitive verbs like arrive, happen, or die passive because they don't have an object to move to the front of the sentence.

Third, always use the past participle form. Don't say the window was break; say the window was broken. 

Lastly, if you mention the agent, make sure to use the correct pronoun case. If the agent is she, it should become by her, not by she.

Practice these patterns, and you will find the passive voice becomes second nature in no time. Keep up the great work!
