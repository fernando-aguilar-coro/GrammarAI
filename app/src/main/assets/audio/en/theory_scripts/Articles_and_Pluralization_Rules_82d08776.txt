Hello! Today, we are going to master the building blocks of English noun phrases: articles and pluralization. Getting these right is the secret to sounding much more natural when you speak.

Let’s start with articles. We use a, an, and the to specify nouns. Use a before singular countable nouns that start with a consonant sound, like a book. Interestingly, even though university starts with a vowel letter, it starts with a consonant sound, so we say a university. Use an before words starting with a vowel sound, like an apple. Remember, even if a word starts with a silent letter, like hour, we use an because the first sound is a vowel. Finally, we use the for specific nouns that both the speaker and listener know, like the sun or the keys. Pronounce it as the before consonants, and thee before vowels.

Next, let’s look at plurals. For most nouns, you simply add an s, like cat becoming cats. If a noun ends in s, ss, sh, ch, x, or z, add es, like bus becoming buses or watch becoming watches. If a word ends in a consonant followed by y, drop the y and add ies, like city becoming cities. But, if there is a vowel before the y, just add an s, like boy becoming boys.

Some words are irregular and don't follow these rules. You just have to memorize them! For example, man becomes men, and tooth becomes teeth. Some words change their suffix, like child becoming children, and some words, like sheep, stay exactly the same in their plural form.

Before we finish, watch out for these common mistakes. Never say an university; it is a university because of the consonant sound. Also, don't forget your articles when talking about professions. Always say she is an engineer, not she is engineer. And finally, avoid adding an s to irregular plurals. It is children, not childs, and teeth, not tooths.

Keep practicing these patterns, and you will be speaking with much more precision in no time. You are doing great!
