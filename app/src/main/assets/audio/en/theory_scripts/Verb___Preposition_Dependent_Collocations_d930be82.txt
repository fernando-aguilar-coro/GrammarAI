Hello there! Today, we are going to master a very important part of English grammar called dependent collocations. These are simply verbs that are permanently linked to specific prepositions. Think of them as a team; they work best when they stay together.

The basic formula is quite simple: you take your verb, add the correct preposition, and then follow it with your object, which could be a noun, a pronoun, or a gerund ending in ing.

Let’s look at some common pairs you will use every day. First, we have listen to. For example, please listen to the instructions carefully. Next is depend on, as in, success depends on hard work. Then we have wait for, like when we say, we are waiting for the bus. And finally, belong to, such as, this book belongs to me.

When you speak, try to use connected speech to sound more natural. Instead of saying each word separately, link the final sound of the verb to the preposition. For instance, in wait for, let the t sound flow right into the f. In depend on, connect the d sound at the end of depend directly to the o in on. It should sound like one smooth unit.

Now, let’s watch out for a few common mistakes. The biggest one is omitting the preposition because of a direct translation from your native language. Always remember to say listen to me, not just listen me. Also, be careful not to mix up your prepositions. For example, it is always depend on, never depend of.

Another important rule involves gerunds. If you have a verb following the preposition, it must end in ing. So, we say I look forward to meeting you, never I look forward to meet you.

Finally, never drop the preposition when an object is present. Keeping that bridge between the verb and the object is what makes your English sound accurate and professional.

Practice these pairs out loud today, and you will find your speech becoming much more fluid and natural. Keep up the great work, and happy learning!
