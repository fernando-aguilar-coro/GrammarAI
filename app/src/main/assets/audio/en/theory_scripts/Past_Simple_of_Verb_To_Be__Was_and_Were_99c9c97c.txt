Hello and welcome! Today, we are mastering one of the most essential building blocks in English: the past simple of the verb to be. 

We use this verb to talk about states, emotions, locations, or identities that happened at a specific time in the past. Unlike most English verbs, the verb to be does not need helping verbs like did. Instead, it has two unique past forms: was, pronounced woz, and were, pronounced wer.

Choosing the right form is simple once you know your subject. 

When your subject is singular, such as I, he, she, or it, we use was. For example: I was tired yesterday, or She was a doctor. 

When your subject is plural, or when you are using the pronoun you, we use were. So, for the second person singular and plural, we say: You were late for class. For a group including yourself, we say: We were at home. And for third-person plural, we say: They were happy.

Now, let us look at negative sentences. To make a negative statement, simply add the word not right after the verb. In casual conversation and everyday writing, we almost always contract these words. 

Was not becomes wasn't. For example: He wasn't ready. Notice that smooth two-syllable rhythm: woz-uhnt. 

Were not becomes weren't. For example: They weren't present. Here, listen closely to the one-syllable sound: wernt. 

What about asking questions? In English questions with the verb to be, we use inversion. That simply means we flip the subject and the verb. We place was or were at the very beginning of the sentence. 

For instance, instead of saying she was at the meeting, we ask: Was she at the meeting? Or, instead of you were surprised, we ask: Were you surprised by the news?

Before we finish, let us review three common traps to avoid. 

First, never use the helper verb did with the verb to be. Learners sometimes ask, Did you be happy? That is incorrect. Always invert the verb directly and ask: Were you happy?

Second, be careful with irregular plural nouns. Words like children, people, or mice are plural, even though they do not end with the letter S. Always use were with them. Say, The children were loud, never the children was loud.

And finally, remember to match your singular subjects with was. Even though it is tempting to mix them up, always say I was there, never I were there.

Take your time practicing these forms out loud. You are doing fantastic work, and with just a little practice, using was and were will feel completely natural!
