Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to learn how to talk about things or people that existed in the past using there was and there were.

The main rule is very simple: your choice depends entirely on whether the noun that follows is singular or plural.

When you are talking about a single item, or an uncountable noun like water or time, use there was. For example, you can say, There was a book on the table, or There was some water in the glass.

On the other hand, when you are talking about two or more things, use there were. For instance, There were three cats in the garden, or There were many people at the conference.

Now, let's look at negatives and questions. To make a negative statement, simply add not. In casual conversation, we usually contract these. There was not becomes there wasn't. And there were not becomes there weren't.

To ask a yes-no question, just flip the word order around and put the verb first. Instead of there was, ask, Was there a doctor on the flight? And instead of there were, ask, Were there any problems during the test?

Let's cover a few common mistakes to watch out for.

First, when you are listing items, match the verb to the very first noun right after it. Even if you list multiple things, if the first noun is singular, use was. Say, There was a pen and two books.

Second, do not confuse the existence word there with the possessive word their. Always use T-H-E-R-E for existence and location.

Third, remember to use proper plural forms after there were. Do not say there were childs; say, There were children.

And finally, never skip the word there! In English, we always need this subject. So avoid saying, Was a great party yesterday, and instead say, There was a great party yesterday.
