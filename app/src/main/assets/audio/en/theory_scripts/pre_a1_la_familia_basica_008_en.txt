Welcome to today's English lesson. Today, we are going to learn essential words to talk about the people closest to us: our family members. By the end of this lesson, you will feel confident introducing and talking about your own family in English.

Let us start with the word father. A father is a male parent. In everyday casual conversation, people often say dad instead of father. When you pronounce father, pay close attention to the sound in the middle, spelled with the letters T and H. It makes a soft, voiced buzzing sound, just like in the words this and that. For example, you can say: My father lives in Chicago, or casually, My dad is very kind.

Next, we have mother. A mother is a female parent. Mother has that exact same voiced sound in the middle. Also, notice the vowel sound at the start. It sounds like the word cup or sun, so we say mother, not maw-ther. In daily speech, most native speakers use the warm, informal word mom, or mum in British English. For instance, you could say: Her mother is a doctor, or simply, My mom makes delicious soup. Together, your father and mother are called your parents.

Now, let us talk about siblings. If you have a male sibling who shares your parents, that person is your brother. Once again, listen for that smooth sound in the middle: brother. Here is an example: I have one older brother.

If you have a female sibling, the word is sister. Pay attention to the clear, crisp S sound at the beginning and in the middle: sister. For example, you might say: My sister loves reading books, or, I have two sisters.

Finally, let us look at the word baby. A baby is a very young child, usually an infant under one year old. Pronounce it smoothly as bay-bee, placing gentle stress on the first syllable. If you are talking about more than one, the plural is babies. A practical example would be: The baby is sleeping peacefully right now.

To practice, think about your own family. Try saying these words out loud in a complete sentence: This is my father, this is my mother, this is my brother, this is my sister, and this is the baby.
