Hello there! Today, we are going to master English quantifiers. These are the words we use before nouns to tell us how much or how many of something we have. The secret to using them correctly is knowing whether your noun is countable or uncountable.

Countable nouns are things you can count individually, like apples or friends. Uncountable nouns are things you cannot count, like water, money, or information.

Let’s look at how we use these words. When you are asking a question or making a negative statement, we use many for countable nouns and much for uncountable nouns. For example, you might ask, Do you have many friends? or Do you have much time? Remember, pronounce many as men-ee and much as mutch.

In positive, affirmative sentences, native speakers prefer to use a lot of, which works for both countable and uncountable nouns. So, instead of saying I have much time, say I have a lot of time. It sounds much more natural!

Now, let’s talk about small amounts. If you have a small but sufficient amount, use a few for countable nouns and a little for uncountable nouns. For instance, I have a few dollars, so we can buy coffee. 

But be careful! If you drop the a, the meaning changes completely. Saying I have few dollars means you have almost none, which is a negative, sad situation. The same applies to little. A little means you have some, but little means you have almost none.

A common mistake is using much with plural countable nouns. Never say I ate much apples; always say I ate many apples. Also, remember that words like advice, furniture, and information are uncountable. You cannot have many advices; you have a lot of advice.

To wrap up, remember that in casual conversation, a lot of is your best friend for positive sentences. Save many and much for your questions and negative statements. Keep practicing these, and you will sound more fluent every single day. You are doing a great job!
