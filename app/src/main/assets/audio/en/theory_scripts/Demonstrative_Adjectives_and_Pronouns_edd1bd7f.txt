Hello there! Today, we are going to master demonstrative adjectives and pronouns. These are the words we use to point to specific things, helping us show how close or far away an object is in space or time.

Let’s look at our four key words. When something is near to you, use this for a single item, like this book, or these for multiple items, like these pens. When something is further away, we use that for a single item, like that car, or those for multiple items, like those trees.

A quick tip on pronunciation: when you say this, keep the vowel short and sharp. When you say these, make sure to stretch that e sound out a bit longer.

Now, these words have two main jobs. First, they can act as adjectives. In this case, they sit right before a noun to describe it. For example, you might say, This book is fascinating, or Those cars are expensive. Notice how the demonstrative is directly modifying the noun that follows it.

Second, they can act as pronouns. This happens when the demonstrative stands all by itself, replacing the noun entirely. For instance, you could say, This is a difficult problem, or Those were the best days of my life. In these cases, there is no noun immediately following the demonstrative.

Beyond physical distance, you can also use these words to talk about time. Use this and these for things happening right now, like saying, This year has been incredible. Use that and those for things in the past or memories that feel further away, like, That was a wonderful summer.

A few common traps to avoid: first, watch your pluralization. Never say this books; always match your demonstrative to the noun, so it should be these books. Second, be careful not to use a demonstrative pronoun as an adjective. You wouldn't say this book is mine by saying this is book is mine. Just pick one: either use it as an adjective before the noun, or as a pronoun standing alone.

Practice these in your daily conversations, and you will sound much more natural and precise. Keep up the great work!
