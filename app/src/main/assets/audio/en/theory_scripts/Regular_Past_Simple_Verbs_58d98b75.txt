Hello there! Today, we are going to master the past simple tense for regular verbs. This is the tense we use to talk about actions that were completed in the past. To make a regular verb past tense, we generally just add the suffix -ed to the end of the word.

Let’s look at the spelling rules. For most verbs, like walk, you simply add -ed to get walked, as in, she walked to the store yesterday. If a verb already ends in an e, like live, you only need to add a d to get lived. If a verb ends in a consonant followed by a y, like study, you change the y to an i and add -ed, making it studied. Finally, for short verbs that follow a consonant-vowel-consonant pattern, like stop, you double the final consonant and add -ed, so it becomes stopped.

Now, pronunciation is where it gets interesting. The -ed ending has three different sounds. First, we have the /t/ sound. This happens after unvoiced sounds like p, k, or s. For example, worked sounds like work-t, and laughed sounds like laugh-t. Second, we have the /d/ sound, used after voiced sounds like m, n, or l. So, played ends with a soft d, and cleaned ends with a soft d sound. Third, we have the /id/ sound. This is the only time you add an extra syllable, and it happens when the verb ends in a t or d sound, like wanted or ended.

A very common mistake is adding that extra syllable to every word. Remember, walked is just one syllable, not walk-id! Also, be careful with your spelling. Don't forget to double that final consonant in words like stopped, and remember that if a verb ends in a vowel plus y, like play, you keep the y and just add -ed to get played.

Practice these sounds out loud, and you will be sounding like a natural in no time. Keep up the great work, and I will see you in the next lesson!
