Hello there! Today, we are going to master reflexive pronouns. These are words that end in self for the singular, or selves for the plural. We use them whenever the person doing the action is the same person receiving the action.

Let’s look at how these match up with our subject pronouns. When the subject is I, we use myself, like in the sentence, I cut myself while cooking. For you, singular, we use yourself, as in, Did you hurt yourself? When we talk about he, we use himself, like he looked at himself in the mirror. For she, we use herself, such as, she baked the cake herself. For it, we use itself, like the machine turned itself off.

When we move to the plural, we use the selves ending. For we, we use ourselves, like we enjoyed ourselves at the party. For you, plural, we use yourselves, as in, please make yourselves at home. And finally, for they, we use themselves, like they defended themselves bravely.

Now, keep in mind that these pronouns have two jobs. Sometimes they are reflexive, meaning they are the object of the verb, like when he taught himself to play guitar. Other times, they are intensive, which means they are just there for emphasis. For example, if I say, the manager himself signed the document, I am just emphasizing the manager. You could take that word out, and the sentence would still make perfect sense.

There are a few common traps to avoid. First, do not use reflexive pronouns when talking about physical accompaniment. If you bring a friend along, say he brought his brother with him, not with himself. 

Second, watch out for compound objects. If you are giving a book to John and me, say exactly that—to John and me—rather than using myself. 

Third, always check your number agreement. Make sure you are using yourself for one person and yourselves for a group. 

Finally, in standard English, we often skip reflexive pronouns when talking about daily grooming. Instead of saying he washed himself, it is much more natural to simply say he washed every morning.

Practice these today, and you will sound much more natural and precise in your English conversations. You are doing a great job!
