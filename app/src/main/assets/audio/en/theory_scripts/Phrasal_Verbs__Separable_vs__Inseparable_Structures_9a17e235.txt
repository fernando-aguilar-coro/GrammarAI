Hello there! Today, we are diving into the world of phrasal verbs. You know, those tricky combinations where a verb joins a particle, like an adverb or a preposition, to create a brand-new meaning. The big question is: can we split them apart, or must they stay together? Let’s break it down.

First, we have separable phrasal verbs. With these, you have a choice. You can place the object after the particle, like saying, she turned off the light. Or, you can tuck the object right in the middle, like, she turned the light off. Both are perfectly correct!

However, there is one golden rule you must remember when using pronouns like it, him, her, or them. If your object is a pronoun, it must go in the middle. You would say, please fill it out, but never please fill out it. That sounds a bit unnatural to a native speaker’s ear, so keep those pronouns tucked inside the verb and particle.

Next, we have inseparable phrasal verbs. These are the ones that refuse to be split. The verb and the particle must stay side-by-side, and the object always follows them. For example, you would say, he ran into an old friend. Even if you use a pronoun, it stays at the end. You would say, he ran into her, never he ran her into.

Now, keep in mind that some phrasal verbs are transitive, meaning they need an object, while others are intransitive, meaning they don’t take an object at all. Intransitive verbs like wake up or break down are always inseparable simply because there is no object to put in the middle.

A common pitfall to watch out for is trying to force a split where it doesn't belong. For instance, don't try to put a long phrase in the middle of an inseparable verb. Always keep look after together when you are looking after someone. Also, remember that three-word phrasal verbs are almost always inseparable.

Learning these patterns takes a little practice, but once you get the rhythm of where the object belongs, your English will sound much more fluid and natural. Keep practicing, and don't be afraid to make mistakes—that is exactly how you learn! You are doing great.
