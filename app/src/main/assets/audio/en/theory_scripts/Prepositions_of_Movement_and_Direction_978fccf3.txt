Hello there! Today, we are going to master prepositions of movement and direction. These are the words we use to describe how people or objects move from one place to another. Unlike static prepositions that tell us where something is, these words describe dynamic action.

Let’s start with five essential prepositions. 

First, we have into, pronounced in-too. We use this when entering a three-dimensional enclosed space. For example, you might say, she walked into the conference room. 

Next is onto, pronounced on-too. This describes moving to the top surface of something, like when a cat jumps onto the kitchen counter. 

Then we have across, pronounced uh-kross. Use this for moving from one side to the other of a flat, two-dimensional surface, such as when they sailed across the wide ocean. 

Fourth is through, pronounced throo. This is for moving from one side to the other of an enclosed space, like a train rushing through a dark tunnel. 

Finally, we have along, pronounced uh-long. This describes moving in a line beside a path or road, like when we strolled along the sandy beach.

It is helpful to remember the difference between into and through. Into means you are entering a space to stay inside, while through means you are passing all the way from one side to the other. 

Also, be careful with onto and across. Onto is for landing on a surface, while across is for crossing an open expanse. 

Now, let’s look at a few common mistakes to avoid. 

First, don't confuse into with in. If you are moving toward the interior of a house, say he ran into the house, not he ran in the house. 

Second, remember that onto is for movement. If a book is already sitting still, don't say it is onto the desk. Just say it is on the desk. 

Third, use across for flat surfaces like streets. It sounds more natural to say she walked across the street rather than through the street. 

The most important thing to remember is that these prepositions are all about motion. By choosing the right word, you add a clear sense of direction and energy to your sentences. Keep practicing, and you will be using these like a native speaker in no time!
