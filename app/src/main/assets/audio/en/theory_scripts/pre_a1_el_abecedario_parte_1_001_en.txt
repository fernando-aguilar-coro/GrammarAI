Welcome to today's lesson on the English alphabet! Whether you are beginning your English journey or brushing up on your pronunciation, mastering the letters is the foundation of clear communication, reading, and spelling.

There are twenty-six letters in the English alphabet, and today we are focusing on the first thirteen: from A to M. Among these first thirteen letters, we will meet three very important vowels: A, E, and I. Let's explore each letter, its name, and how to pronounce it naturally.

First is the letter A. The name of the letter sounds like ay. Because it is a vowel, it can have a short sound, as in apple or cat, or a long sound that matches its name, as in cake and day.

Next is B, pronounced bee. Press your lips together firmly to make the b sound, like in book or baby. Be careful not to confuse it with the softer v sound!

Then we have C, pronounced see. The letter C often makes a hard sound like a k in cat and coffee, but it makes a soft s sound when followed by the letters e, i, or y, as in city or center.

After that comes D, pronounced dee. You make this sound by touching the tip of your tongue to the roof of your mouth, just behind your teeth, as in dog and door.

Our second vowel is E, pronounced ee. Its short sound is eh, like in pen and egg, while its long sound is stretched and smiling, like in meet or tree.

Next is F, pronounced eff. Rest your upper teeth gently on your lower lip to make this soft blowing sound, like in fish and friend.

Then comes G, pronounced jee. G can be hard, as in good and garden, or soft, sounding like a j, as in giraffe and giant.

Letter H is pronounced aitch. A very common mistake is adding an h sound at the beginning of the name. Remember, the letter name starts with a pure vowel sound: aitch. When used in words, it produces a gentle breath of air, as in home and happy.

Our third vowel is I, pronounced eye. Its short sound is ih, like in sit and milk, while its long sound matches its name, as in ice and time.

Next is J, pronounced jay, making the energetic sound in jump and joy.

Then K, pronounced kay, with a crisp sound at the back of the throat, as in kitchen and key.

Next is L, pronounced ell. Make sure the tip of your tongue lifts directly up to the roof of your mouth, like in light and love.

Finally, we reach M, pronounced em. Close both lips fully to hum the sound, as in mother and music.

Take a moment to practice saying these thirteen letters aloud: A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, K, L, M. Great job today! Keep practicing your vowel sounds, and I will see you soon for part two.
