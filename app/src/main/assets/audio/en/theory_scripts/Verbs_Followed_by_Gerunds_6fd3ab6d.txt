Hello and welcome! Today, we are exploring a really important pattern in English grammar: verbs that are followed by gerunds.

When we connect two verbs in a sentence, the second verb cannot always stay in its base form. Very often, it transforms into a gerund, which is simply the base verb ending in i-n-g. Even though it looks like an active verb, a gerund actually functions just like a noun, acting as the direct object of the sentence.

Today, we are focusing on three primary verbs that always take a gerund complement: enjoy, avoid, and suggest.

Let us look at our first verb: enjoy, pronounced enjoy. We use it to express taking pleasure in an activity. The formula is simple: subject, plus enjoy, plus your i-n-g verb. For example: She enjoys reading. 

Our second verb is avoid, pronounced avoid, which means to keep away from something or prevent an action. Following the same pattern, you say: He avoided answering. Notice that answering is the action he stayed away from.

The third verb is suggest, pronounced suggest. When you want to propose an idea or plan, place the gerund immediately after suggest. For example: They suggested leaving early.

Now, what if you need to make the gerund negative? The rule is very straightforward: place the word not directly before the gerund. For instance, you could say: She avoided not replying to the email, or, I suggest not waiting until tomorrow.

Let us address a few common pitfalls so you can speak with complete confidence. 

First and foremost, never use a to-infinitive after these verbs. A very common mistake is saying, I enjoy to swim. That is incorrect. You must say, I enjoy swimming. Similarly, never say, he avoided to answer, or, they suggested to leave. Always stick with the i-n-g form.

Second, be careful with phrasal verbs where to is a preposition rather than an infinitive marker. A great example is look forward to. Because to is a preposition here, it must be followed by a gerund as well. You should say, I look forward to meeting you, never, I look forward to meet you.

Finally, remember never to drop the i-n-g ending. 

Try practicing today by describing your own daily habits. Ask yourself: What do you enjoy doing in your free time? What tasks do you try to avoid doing? With a little consistent practice, using gerunds naturally will become second nature. Keep up the fantastic work!
