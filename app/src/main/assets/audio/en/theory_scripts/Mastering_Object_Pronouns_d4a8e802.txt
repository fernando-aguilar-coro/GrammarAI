Hello there! Today, we are going to master object pronouns. These are essential building blocks in English because they act as the receiver of an action. While subject pronouns perform the action, object pronouns receive it, or follow a preposition.

Let’s look at how these change from their subject forms. 

When you are talking about yourself, use me, pronounced with a long e sound, as in, she gave the book to me. For the second person, you stays the same, like in the sentence, I will call you later. 

For he, the object form is him. You can pronounce it clearly as him, or sometimes in fast speech, just as im. For example, John invited him to the party. For she, we use her, which sounds like her. Think of the sentence, Sarah told her the truth. 

If you are talking about a thing, use it, like the dog chased it. When you are talking about a group including yourself, use us, as in, they invited us for dinner. Finally, for they, use them, which you can pronounce as them or the softer thum. For instance, I saw them at the store.

Remember, you generally place these pronouns in two spots. First, directly after the main verb, like in Mary loves him. Second, after prepositions, such as in the phrase, please sit with us.

Now, let’s watch out for a few common mistakes. A very frequent error is using a subject pronoun after a preposition. Never say give it to I; always say give it to me. Also, be careful not to confuse possessive adjectives with object pronouns. If you are talking about a woman, say I saw her, not I saw she. 

Another tip is to make sure you include the object pronoun when it is needed. If you are using a transitive verb, you need an object, so say she gave it to me, rather than just she gave to me. 

Lastly, watch out for compound objects. If you are inviting two people, say she invited Sarah and him, not she invited Sarah and he. 

Keep practicing these, and you will sound much more natural in your daily conversations. You are doing a great job!
