Hello there! Today, we are going to master the three most common prepositions of time: at, on, and in. Think of these as a pyramid, moving from the most specific point in time to the most general duration.

Let’s start with at, pronounced with a short, crisp sound. We use at for very specific moments, like exact clock times or holiday periods. For example, you might say the train departs at 3:00 PM, or we will meet at midnight. Even though Christmas is a holiday, we use at for that specific period as well.

Next, we have on. We use this for days of the week, specific dates, and special calendar days. So, you would say the conference begins on Monday, or my birthday is on July 4th. Remember, whenever you are talking about a specific day, on is your go-to choice.

Finally, we use in for longer, more general periods of time, like months, years, decades, centuries, or parts of the day. For instance, you might say my birthday is in January, or we are living in 2024. Also, keep in mind that we say in the morning or in the afternoon.

Now, let’s look at a few common mistakes to help you sound more natural. A very frequent error is using in with clock times, like saying in 3 o'clock. Remember, clock times always need at, so it should be at 3 o'clock. Another common slip-up is using at with days of the week. Always use on for days, like on Monday. 

Also, be careful not to use on with months or years alone. If you are just mentioning the month or the year, use in, such as in July or in 1999. 

One final tip: while we say in the morning and in the afternoon, there is one special exception. We always say at night. It is a classic idiom that you should memorize.

To recap: use at for precise clock times, on for days and dates, and in for longer periods like months and years. Keep practicing these, and you will be using them perfectly in no time! You are doing a great job, so keep going.
