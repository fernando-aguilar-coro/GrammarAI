Hello there! Today, we are going to master the phrase going to. You might hear native speakers pronounce it as going to, or more casually, as gonna. We use this phrase when we talk about the future, specifically for things we have already planned or when we see clear evidence that something is about to happen.

To build a sentence, you need three parts: the verb be, the phrase going to, and the base form of your main verb.

Let’s look at how we structure these sentences. For an affirmative statement, you say, for example, She is going to travel to Japan. If you want to make it negative, you simply add not after the verb be. For instance, They are not going to buy a new car. And for questions, you start with the verb be, like this: Are you going to attend the meeting?

Remember, we use going to when we have a prior plan. If you have already decided to renovate your kitchen next month, you say, We are going to renovate our kitchen. We also use it when we see evidence. If you look up and see dark, heavy clouds, you don't just guess; you say, It is going to rain, because you have the evidence right in front of you.

A quick tip for verbs of motion like go or come: you can often simplify your speech. Instead of saying I am going to go to the store, you can just say, I am going to the store.

Now, let’s avoid a few common mistakes. First, never forget the verb be. Don't say I going to study; always say I am going to study. Second, be careful with spontaneous decisions. If the phone rings suddenly, don't say I am going to answer it. That sounds like you planned it! Instead, use will for sudden choices. 

Also, keep your main verb in its simple base form. It is always she is going to visit London, never she is going to visits London. Finally, if you see physical evidence, like snow falling, use going to instead of will. It is going to snow is much more natural than I think it will snow in that moment.

Keep practicing these structures, and you will sound more natural and confident in no time. You are doing great!
