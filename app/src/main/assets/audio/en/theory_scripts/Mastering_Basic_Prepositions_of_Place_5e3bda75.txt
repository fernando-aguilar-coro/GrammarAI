Hello and welcome! Today, we are mastering basic prepositions of place. These little words are essential because they tell us exactly where something is located in relation to something else. 

To choose the right preposition, it helps to think about dimensions. 

Let's start with the word at. We use at for a specific point in space, almost like a dot on a map. Think of it as a zero-dimensional point. For instance, you would say, she is waiting at the bus stop, or meet me at the entrance.

Next, we have on. We use on when an object is touching a flat surface or a line, like a two-dimensional plane. For example, the book is on the table, or the picture is hanging on the wall. 

Then comes in. We use in for enclosed spaces, containers, or three-dimensional volumes where something is surrounded on all sides. For example, the keys are in the drawer, or there is milk in the fridge. 

So, remember this simple hierarchy: at is for a specific point, on is for a surface, and in is for an enclosed space.

Now let us look at three more very useful prepositions. 

First is under. We use under when an object is directly lower than something else. For example, the shoes are under the bed. 

Second is next to. This simply means beside or right alongside something. For instance, the lamp is next to the sofa. 

Third is between. We use between when an object sits in the middle of two distinct things. For example, the cat is sleeping between the two chairs.

Let's review a few common mistakes to watch out for.

First, public transportation. In English, we usually say on the bus, on the train, or on the plane, because these are large vehicles where you can stand up and walk. However, for a smaller vehicle like a personal car or a taxi, we say in the car.

Second, be careful not to overuse at when you are inside a room. Never say at the room. Because a room is an enclosed three-dimensional space, always say in the room.

Third, remember that between requires two reference points. You cannot say the table is between the chair. You must say the table is between the chair and the wall.

And finally, never drop the preposition entirely. Always include it clearly. Say the cat is under the bed, not the cat is the bed.

Take a look around you right now. Try describing three objects in your room using in, on, and at. With a little practice, you will use them naturally and effortlessly. Keep up the wonderful work!
