Welcome to today's lesson. Today, we are going to master a classic grammar challenge in the present perfect tense: the difference between been and gone. 

While both words refer to travel or movement, choosing the wrong one can completely change the meaning of your sentence, especially regarding where someone is right now.

Let's begin with the core distinction. Think of been as a completed round trip. When you use have been or has been, it means the person visited a place and has already returned. The subject is back at their starting point. For example, if you say, She has been to Paris, it means she visited Paris in the past, enjoyed her trip, and is now back home.

On the other hand, think of gone as a one-way trip that is still in progress. When you use have gone or has gone, it means the person traveled to a place and has not returned yet. They are either currently at that destination or still on their way there. For instance, if you say, He has gone to Paris, this means he is in Paris right now, or on the plane heading there. He is definitely not standing here talking to you.

This leads us to a simple rule for asking about life experiences. When you want to ask someone about places they have visited in their lifetime, you should always ask, Have you ever been to, followed by the destination. For example, Have you ever been to Italy? Notice that we do not say, Have you ever gone to Italy? Why? Because if the person had gone there and hadn't returned, they wouldn't be standing in front of you to answer your question!

Because gone means the person is currently away, you will almost always use it with third-person subjects, like he, she, they, or someone's name. You cannot look someone in the eyes and say, I have gone to the supermarket, because you are clearly standing right there.

Now, let's review a few common mistakes to watch out for.

First, be careful with someone's current location. If a coworker is missing from work, do not say, John is not in the office because he has been to the bank. Since John is still at the bank, you must say, He has gone to the bank.

Second, do not forget the preposition to. Many learners accidentally say, She has been Paris. Always remember to include the preposition and say, She has been to Paris.

Finally, remember that been denotes a completed visit. If someone says, They have been to Japan, it does not mean they live there today; it simply means they traveled there and returned.

Keep that picture in your mind: been is a round trip, and gone is still on the way. Practice using both today, and you will sound natural and precise every time.
