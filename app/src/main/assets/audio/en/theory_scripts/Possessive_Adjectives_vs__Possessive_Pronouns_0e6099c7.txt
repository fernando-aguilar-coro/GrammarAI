Hello and welcome! Today, we are going to master one of the most useful areas of English grammar: expressing ownership. Specifically, we will look at the difference between possessive adjectives and possessive pronouns. They look quite similar, but they do completely different jobs in a sentence.

Let us begin with possessive adjectives. Think of these as helping words that always need a noun right after them. They describe who owns that noun, and they can never stand alone. 

Here is how they work:
For the pronoun I, we use my, as in: This is my book.
For you, we use your: Is this your jacket?
For he, we use his: His car is fast.
For she, we use her: Her presentation was clear.
For it, we use its, spelled I-T-S without an apostrophe: The dog wagged its tail.
For we, we use our: Our team won the match.
And for they, we use their: Their house is large.

Notice the golden rule here: every single possessive adjective is followed immediately by a noun, like book, jacket, or house.

Now, let us switch gears to possessive pronouns. Unlike adjectives, possessive pronouns do not sit in front of a noun. Instead, they replace the entire noun phrase to avoid repeating words. Because they stand completely on their own, you will never put a noun right after them. They usually appear at the end of a sentence or after the verb to be.

Let us hear how those same ideas transform:
My book becomes mine: This book is mine.
Your jacket becomes yours: Is this jacket yours?
His car becomes his: That fast car is his. Notice that his stays exactly the same.
Her presentation becomes hers: The clear presentation was hers.
Our team becomes ours: The winning team is ours.
And their house becomes theirs: The large house is theirs.
Keep in mind that the word it does not have a standard possessive pronoun form in modern English.

Before we finish, let us review a few common pitfalls that even advanced learners encounter.

First, watch out for its versus it's. Without an apostrophe, I-T-S shows possession, as in its tail. With an apostrophe, I-T-apostrophe-S is simply a contraction for it is or it has.

Second, remember never to put a noun directly after a possessive pronoun. Saying, this is yours book, is incorrect. You should say, this is your book, or, this book is yours.

Third, never leave a possessive adjective stranded by itself. You cannot say, this book is my. It must be, this book is mine.

Finally, possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe. Words like yours, hers, ours, and theirs are always written without one.

Keep practicing these patterns in your daily speaking, and choosing the right word will soon feel completely natural. You are doing fantastic work, and I will see you in the next lesson!
