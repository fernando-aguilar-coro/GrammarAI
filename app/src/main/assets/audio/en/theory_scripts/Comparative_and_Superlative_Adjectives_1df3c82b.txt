Hello everyone, and welcome to today's lesson! Today, we are going to explore comparative and superlative adjectives. 

Adjectives help us describe the world around us, and when we want to compare different things, we change their forms. We use comparative adjectives when comparing two things, and superlative adjectives when comparing three or more things.

Let's start with regular adjectives and see how their forms change depending on their spelling and syllable count.

First, for short, one-syllable adjectives like "fast," we simply add -e-r for the comparative, making it "faster," and -e-s-t for the superlative, making it "fastest."

Second, if a short adjective already ends in a silent e, like "large," you just add -r or -s-t. So, "large" becomes "larger" and "largest."

Third, pay close attention to short adjectives that end with a single vowel followed by a single consonant, like "big." For these, double that final consonant before adding your ending! So, "big" becomes "bigger," spelled b-i-g-g-e-r, and "biggest."

Fourth, for adjectives ending in a consonant plus y, like "happy," remember to change that y to an i before adding -e-r or -e-s-t. So, "happy" becomes "happier" and "happiest."

Finally, what about longer adjectives with two or more syllables? Words like "careful" do not take -e-r or -e-s-t. Instead, we place the word "more" or "most" in front of them. For instance, "careful" becomes "more careful" and "most careful."

Now, let's look at irregular adjectives. These do not follow standard rules, so you will need to memorize them.

For example, "good" becomes "better" in the comparative and "best" in the superlative.
"Bad" changes completely to "worse" and "worst."
And "far" becomes "further" or "farther," and "furthest" or "farthest."

Before we wrap up, let's go over a few common mistakes to avoid.

First, watch out for double comparatives! Never say "more faster." Since "fast" already ends in -e-r, just say "faster."

Second, when comparing two specific things in a sentence, always use the word "than" after the comparative adjective. For example, "She is taller than him."

Third, always use the definite article "the" before superlative adjectives. For example, "He is the tallest boy in the class."

And lastly, don't forget to change that y to an i when spelling words like "happier."
