Hello there! Today, we are going to master the present perfect tense by using three very helpful adverbs: just, already, and yet. These words help us explain exactly when something happened in relation to the present moment.

Let’s start with the word just, which is pronounced with a soft j sound at the beginning. We use just to talk about actions that were completed a very short time ago. You should place it right between the auxiliary verb have or has and the main verb. For example, you could say, I have just finished my report.

Next, we have already. This word is used when something happened sooner than we expected. Like just, it sits comfortably between the auxiliary verb and the main verb. Think of it as a way to show surprise at how quickly something was done. For instance, you might say, She has already booked the tickets.

Finally, let’s look at yet. We use yet in negative sentences and questions to talk about things that haven't happened yet but are expected to. Unlike the other two, yet always goes at the very end of the sentence. For example, you can ask, Have you finished your homework yet? Or, in a negative sentence, you might say, They haven't arrived yet.

Now, here are a few common pitfalls to watch out for. First, remember that yet should never be used in the middle of an affirmative sentence; it belongs only at the end of negatives or questions. Second, try not to confuse just and already. Just is about recency, meaning it happened a moment ago, while already is about speed or anticipation.

Also, be careful not to drop your auxiliary verb. Even if you are using an adverb, you still need to include have or has before it. Finally, while you might hear people put already at the end of a sentence in casual conversation, it is much better to keep it in the middle of the sentence for standard, clear English.

Practice these today by thinking of three things you have just done, one thing you have already accomplished, and one thing you haven't finished yet. You are doing a great job, so keep practicing and keep speaking!
